Governing equations

The model was developed from the solver pimpleFoam. The hydrodynamics is solved using the PIMPLE algorithm for incompressible fluid. Some sediments are transported in suspension, they are advected by the fluid, settle under the influence of gravity and are mixed by turbulence. A erosion/deposition term controls the exchange between the suspended load and the sediment bed. The rest of the sediments are transported as bedload, they slide, roll and saltate on the bed. These different sediment fluxes lead to the morphological evolution described by the Exner equation and handled by the Arbitrary Lagrangian Eulerian (ALE) approach. With this approach, the sediment bed acts as a boundary of the computational domain. The mesh is dynamic and gets distorted in time so that the bed boundary matches the geometry changes.

Arbitrary Lagrangian Eulerian (ALE) Approach

The ALE method is used to handle the moving bed boundary. In this framework, the mesh is deformed to follow the bed evolution, while the flow equations are solved in a moving reference frame.

This flux treatment is validated by a mass-conservation test on a 1D moving-bed sedimentation case (movingBed1Dsedim tutorial): the relative mass balance error stays below ~0.2% with both terms of correctly relativized, against a drift of up to ~9% if the mesh-motion contribution of either term is omitted.

PIMPLE-Exner Sub-Iterations

For improved coupling between hydrodynamics and morphodynamics, sedExnerFoam supports optional sub-iterations between the PIMPLE loop and the Exner equation. This feature allows the flow and bed to converge together within a single time step, avoiding the one-time-step lag between flow and bed updates. The sub-iteration process includes:

  • Storage of bed state (bedload flux and bed points displacement) at the start of each time step (see storeBedState.H)
  • Evaluation of bed shear stress, bedload, suspension, and Exner equation at every PIMPLE outer iteration
  • Under-relaxation of the bed level increment dH across sub-iterations (controlled by PIMPLE/zbRelax)
  • Convergence check based on the residual of bed level changes (controlled by PIMPLE/zbTol)

This feature is activated when PIMPLE/nOuterCorrectors > 1. With nOuterCorrectors = 1 (default), the behavior is unchanged from previous versions.

Bed Motion Time Step Constraint

To prevent mesh cell inversion when the bed moves rapidly relative to the first fluid cell height, sedExnerFoam includes a bed Courant number constraint. The time step is bounded such that the bed displacement remains under bedloadProperties/maxCoBed (default 0.3) times the local first fluid cell height. This constraint uses the converged bed displacement from the previous time step and is reactive, similar to the standard Courant number constraint.

For more details, the interested reader is referred to Renaud et al. (2026) .

Known Numerical Limitations

Grid-scale bed oscillations

The coupling between the flow, the bedload flux and the Exner equation (eq2) on a moving mesh is prone to grid-to-grid (checkerboard-like) oscillations of the bed level, analogous to the pressure-velocity decoupling that motivates Rhie-Chow interpolation in collocated incompressible CFD. Through this feedback loop, the shear stress driving is computed from the velocity gradient on the current (deformed) mesh, which itself depends on the bed level from the previous step: nothing in a purely centered, non-dissipative discretisation prevents one cell from rising while its neighbour falls.

The PIMPLE-Exner sub-iteration (PIMPLE-Exner Sub-Iterations) resynchronises the flow and the bed in time within a time step; it does not, by itself, control this spatial decoupling mechanism, which can persist (or appear differently) with or without sub-iterations enabled.

The following numerical controls, all available in bedloadProperties (see File bedloadProperties in Input Parameters), affect the amplitude of these oscillations:

  • qbScheme (sub-dictionary exnerAdvection): a centered interpolation of on faMesh edges is the most direct source of grid-to-grid decoupling; upwind introduces the numerical diffusion needed to recouple neighbouring faces.
  • ABorder: the first-order Adams-Bashforth integration of the Exner equation is weakly unstable when combined with a centered bedload flux; the explicit Euler scheme (ABorder=0) is more dissipative and more robust for a given noise amplitude.
  • filterExner / filterShields: explicit diffusive filters (see filter.H) specifically intended to damp grid-scale noise in dH / the Shields number without affecting the large-scale trend, when the two controls above are not sufficient on their own.
  • Saturation length Lsat (see Bedload saturation (sub-dictionary 'saturation') in Input Parameters): a length shorter than the local mesh spacing makes the bedload saturation problem ill-posed and tends to amplify high-frequency noise rather than filter it.
  • Mesh quality: once the ALE motion solver has accumulated distortion near the bed (see Bed Motion Time Step Constraint above), the velocity gradient itself becomes noisy, which propagates into the Shields number, and dH even when the flux scheme is otherwise appropriate.

A practical first diagnostic is to compare a run using qbScheme=upwind and ABorder=0 against the same case with the configured settings: if the oscillation disappears, it is primarily a spatial/temporal scheme issue; if it persists, the saturation length and mesh quality should be investigated instead.
